Health Insurance

What new advisors need to know to guide students through the visa health-insurance requirement.

What you need to know

Health insurance is mandatory for the student visa and for the entire duration of a student's stay in Spain.

To be valid for the visa, the policy must tick four boxes:

  • Full coverage across health services
  • Repatriation services included
  • No co-payments
  • No grace periods

Students often ask whether travel insurance counts. It does not. That is the answer to give, every time.

IE has agreements with three Spanish providers. All of them offer IE-student discounts and can issue policies before the student arrives:

Adeslas Cigna Sanitas

Know these names by heart so you can point students in the right direction without hesitation.

💡 Quick tips

  • Remind students that insurance is not included in their program fees. It is an extra cost they must plan for.
  • Encourage students to contact providers directly; all three partners are used to onboarding IE students.

Long-Term Accommodation

For full-time master students settling in Madrid for the whole programme. What new advisors need to know to guide them through the housing market.

What you need to know

Beyond Campus is the IE-trusted housing platform built by alumni, it is the first place to send students. It connects landlords directly with IE students and lists residences such as MasterHome, FunWay and The Residence.

Recommended real-estate agencies often give IE-student discounts on commission, save these contacts for warm referrals:

Dflat PYR Solutions Antoran Accommadrid

The open-market portals students use the most, know each one by name so you can guide their searches:

Idealista Enalquiler Fotocasa

Affordable, well-connected neighbourhoods near campus to recommend:

Almagro Chamberí Cuatro Caminos Goya Guindalera Lista Prosperidad

Memorise these so you can answer "where should I live?" without hesitation.

Before any student signs or pays, walk them through these non-negotiables:

  • Visit the flat in person, never pay a deposit on a flat they have not seen

  • Read the contract carefully and confirm what is included before signing

💡 Quick tips

  • Encourage students to view the flat at both daytime and evening, noise levels often change after dark.

Public Transportation in Madrid

What new advisors need to know to guide students through Madrid's transport system.

What you need to know

Madrid's public transport is run by the Consorcio Regional de Transportes (CRTM). Students living in Madrid Capital only need Zone A. This is a common source of confusion, so be ready to reassure them.

There are two physical cards advisors must know inside-out:

  • TTP (Personal Public Transport Card): essential for residents. It is the only card that accepts the much cheaper Monthly Travel Pass, and it takes 7-10 days to arrive.
  • Multi Card: holds 10-trip Metrobús tickets. Recommend it for the first weeks and for visitors.

Memorise the closest metro stops to María de Molina campus:

  • Gregorio Marañón (L7/L10): 2 minutes
  • Avenida de América (L4/6/7/9): 5-8 minutes
  • Nuevos Ministerios (L6/8/10 + Cercanías): 10-12 minutes, direct to the airport via L8

💡 Quick tips

  • Remind under-26 students that they qualify for the youth Abono Transporte. It is significantly discounted and often missed.
  • Always tell students to order the TTP early. There is a wait, and without it they cannot load the cheap monthly pass.
  • Use the Multi Card as your default recommendation for weeks 1-2 before the TTP arrives.

Initial Fingerprinting (Non-EU NIE)

What new advisors need to know to guide non-EU students through the fingerprinting appointment that starts NIE card issuance.

What you need to know

Within a student's first weeks in Spain they must attend a fingerprinting appointment to start NIE card issuance. Know this document list cold, it is the most common source of student questions:

  • Appointment confirmation (printed)
  • EX17 form, original + copy
  • Passport: main page, visa page, entry stamp (+ copies)
  • Enrolment certificate from IE
  • Recent ID photo (32x26mm, white background)
  • Padrón certificate, issued within the last 90 days
  • 16,08 € tax (form 790-012), paid at a bank or ATM in the same calendar year as the appointment

Watch for the two most common edge cases:

  • Schengen transit: if the student transited through another Schengen country and has no Spanish entry stamp, they must bring their boarding pass
  • Family members: same documents plus a copy of the student's fingerprinting certificate (or NIE card) and a copy of the student's visa

After the appointment students get a stamped certificate proving NIE processing, tell them to keep the original safe at home and carry a copy. It is NOT a valid travel document, travel outside Spain requires an Authorization to Return (ATR).

The physical NIE card is picked up at the same police station 30–50 days later, in person, no proxies allowed.

💡 Quick tips

  • Always remind students to arrive early, the police station gets crowded fast.
  • Ask students to send a copy of both sides of their NIE card to student.services@ie.edu once they receive it.
  • If a student plans to travel during the NIE process, immediately refer them to the ATR guide.

Picking up the NIE Card

What new advisors need to know to walk students through the second NIE appointment, the physical card pick-up.

What you need to know

Roughly 30 to 50 days after the fingerprinting appointment, the student must return to collect their physical NIE card. Two things to make crystal clear:

  • The pick-up must happen at the same office where the fingerprinting appointment took place. No other police station can hand the card over.
  • The student must book a separate appointment for the pick-up, walk-ins are not accepted.

To attend, the student needs to bring three documents, no exceptions:

  • The printed appointment confirmation
  • The original passport
  • The certificate of having attended the fingerprinting appointment

If any of those are missing, the appointment will be rejected and the student will need to rebook, so it is worth checking each item with them in advance.

💡 Quick tips

  • Remind students to print the appointment confirmation, a phone screenshot is not always accepted.
  • Tell students to keep the fingerprinting certificate safe from day one, they will need it again here.
  • Once the card is collected, ask students to email a copy of both sides to student.services@ie.edu so the file stays complete.

What new advisors need to know to guide EU students through their mandatory registration.

The EU NIE booking instructions are maintained by Student Services on docs.ie.edu and link out to the official appointment portal, always work from the live document so you hand students the current links.

What you need to know

EU citizens don't need a visa, but they must process the Certificado de Registro / European NIE card, it is mandatory for stays over three months.

The key facts to keep in your head:

  • Single appointment, documents reviewed on the spot

  • After the appointment, ask the student to send a copy of the certificate to Student Services so the file stays complete

💡 Quick tips

  • Remind students to bring originals AND copies of every document, missing copies is the most common reason appointments are rejected.

Padrón (City Hall Registration)

What new advisors need to know to guide students through the padrón, the address registration that unlocks almost every other procedure.

What you need to know

The empadronamiento is a mandatory, free registration with Madrid City Hall and the certificate is issued on the spot. It gates the NIE, DNI, social security and other procedures, so it is one of the first things you will advise on.

Standard document set for the appointment, know the list so you can check a student's paperwork at a glance:

  • Proof of appointment (printed)
  • Original passport + copy
  • Proof of permanent address (housing contract), original + copy
  • Completed registration application form

If the student does not hold the contract (shared flat, lives with someone else), the contract holder must authorise them. This scenario trips students up often, be ready to walk them through it:

  • Copy of the contract
  • Recent utility bill in the holder's name
  • Copy of the holder's ID / NIE
  • Form signed in the AUTHORIZER section
  • Ideally a short authorisation letter

Memorise the nearest OAC offices so you can direct students based on where they live:

Salamanca · Velázquez 52 Chamartín · Príncipe de Vergara 142 Tetuán · Simancas 6 Chamberí · Reina Victoria 7

Salamanca and Chamartín are closest to María de Molina, Tetuán and Chamberí are closest to IE Tower.

💡 Quick tips

  • Tell students to book the earliest available slot, appointment availability is the single biggest bottleneck.
  • The padrón certificate must be issued within 90 days before the fingerprinting appointment, expired padrones mean re-requesting.
  • For shared flats, always check the authoriser documents first, this is where most student cases fall apart.

DNI (Spanish Identity Card)

What new advisors need to know to guide Spanish-national students through DNI issuance.

What you need to know

Spanish nationals who don't yet have a DNI must process it once in Spain. This is a niche case but it comes up, be ready to explain the route without sending the student down the wrong path.

The key document is the Certificación literal de nacimiento from the Civil Registry (or the Certificate of Spanish Nationality). Watch the rules closely:

  • Issued within the last 6 months
  • Must carry the note that it was issued specifically for DNI purposes
  • Missing that note is a common rejection reason

The process essentials to keep in mind:

  • A first-time DNI cannot be booked online, students must walk in to a DNI issuing office in Madrid
  • Padrón registration is a prerequisite, always check the student has that first
  • Direct students to the official DNI website for the current full document list

💡 Quick tips

  • Advise students to contact the Spanish Consulate well in advance about the certificación literal, it can take weeks.
  • Always verify the padrón is in place before the DNI attempt, walking in without it wastes the student's trip.
  • Point students to the official DNI website for the full document list so they have the current requirements.

Authorization to Return (ATR)

What new advisors need to know to guide students who want to travel while their NIE is in process or being renewed.

What you need to know

The ATR allows re-entry to Spain only, NOT to other Schengen countries. Own this distinction, it is one of the most common and most dangerous student misunderstandings.

Booking flow: official portal → Madrid → AUTORIZACIÓN DE REGRESO. The recommended station is Avenida de los Poblados 51 (metro Aluche, L5), but any available office works.

Documents for initial students (those whose first NIE is in process):

  • Appointment confirmation (printed)
  • EX13 form, original + copy
  • Passport: main + visa pages (+ copies)
  • Initial fingerprinting receipt
  • 10,72 € tax (form 790-012), paid at a bank or ATM in the same calendar year
  • Plane tickets recommended

Documents for renewing students (NIE card already held, renewal in process), the same basics plus the previous / current NIE card and one of:

  • Stamped EX00 or renewal receipt
  • Renewal resolution letter
  • Printed "en trámite" status
  • Renewal fingerprinting receipt

The ATR is usually issued on the spot, valid for 90 days, single re-entry, and re-usable only if it is not stamped at the border.

💡 Quick tips

  • Always warn students: the ATR does NOT let them enter other Schengen countries, they must check transit rules with each consulate.
  • Encourage direct flights back to Spain to avoid Schengen transit issues.
  • If a student has a previous valid ATR, tell them to bring it to the appointment, officials sometimes ask for it.

AES Guide (Madrid)

What new advisors need to know to guide students applying for a student permit from inside Spain (AES).

What you need to know

AES (Autorización de Estancia por Estudios) is the route students use when they did not obtain a visa before coming, they apply from Spain as tourists or from another valid permit. It is a plan-B pathway with heavy documentation and a 5–6 month processing time. Recognise this is a complex case you must flag and track.

To be eligible, the student must be legally in Spain under one of these statuses:

  • Visa-exempt passport
  • Valid Schengen visa
  • Valid Schengen residence permit
  • Valid Spanish permit

The application must be submitted at least 60 days before tourist or resident status expires, any late submission is auto-rejected.

The AES portal and forms change often, so always pull the latest version of the Madrid AES guide from Student Services before advising a student. Never mix instructions from old versions.

💡 Quick tips

  • Remind students they cannot travel outside Spain during AES processing, this often surprises them and changes their plans.
  • Warn students about the risk of rejection: a rejected AES forces them home to apply at a consulate, potentially missing classes.
  • Ask students to notify Student Services if they opt for AES so we can track the case, never let them go it alone.

Renewal Guide (NIE Card)

What new advisors need to know to guide students through the NIE renewal window, late renewals get rejected.

What you need to know

Renewal applications must be submitted in the city where the student will study, if the previous permit was granted elsewhere, they apply in Madrid. Spot the jurisdictional issue early.

The critical submission window is the single most important rule you will enforce, memorise it cold:

  • 60 days BEFORE the NIE card's expiry date (recommended)
  • Up to 90 days AFTER the expiry date (legal cut-off)
  • Beyond 90 days after expiry, submissions are auto-rejected and the student must leave the country

Other rules to keep in mind:

  • The student must be physically in Spain to submit, travel abroad means returning before expiry
  • PDP renewals must be submitted BEFORE starting the internship
  • Online renewal via Mercurio requires an FNMT digital certificate (see prerequisite block below)
  • After approval, a renewal fingerprinting appointment and then card pick-up follow

💡 Quick tips

  • Always tell students to scan every document before submission, originals never come back.
  • If a student is at risk of missing the legal window, escalate to Student Services immediately for alternative submission channels.
  • Proactively ask about upcoming travel, it is a common way renewals get blown.
🔐 Prerequisite: FNMT Digital Certificate

The FNMT digital certificate is the gateway to electronic submissions on RED SARA and the Mercurio renewal portal. Without it, the student cannot file the renewal, the job-search permit, social security, and more. Treat it as a prerequisite, not an accessory.

Recommend the app route: the "FNMT Digital Certificate" app on the phone (the app store region must be set to Spain). Choose "Solicitar Certificado Digital". If the app store isn't set to Spain, the website alternative exists but is harder, flag this to Student Services.

Critical rules students get wrong:

  • A valid NIE card (or approved renewal resolution letter) is mandatory at the time of application
  • Three request options appear in the flow, but ONLY option 2 ("Submit a request in person") applies to non-EU citizens
  • The email confirmation link expires in 24 hours, missing it forces a restart

For identity verification, students choose between:

  • Dirección Provincial de la TGSS, walk-in, 9–14h
  • Any Madrid City Hall office, appointment required

💡 If a student is close to NIE renewal, push them to get the FNMT done NOW while the NIE is still valid, it avoids a messy situation.

Social Security Number

What new advisors need to know to guide students through obtaining a Spanish social security number before working.

What you need to know

IE cannot request social security numbers on the student's behalf, this is their individual responsibility. State this clearly when a student asks you to do it for them.

The number can be requested online or in person and typically resolves in 1-2 weeks. Once approved, the authorities email instructions to download the approval letter.

The standard document set is the same for every route:

  • TA-1 form (available online or in-office)
  • ID document: NIE card / Certificado de Registro / DNI. If the NIE is being renewed, bring proof of the renewal submission too.
  • Signed JMI contract, job contract or internship annex

Online route: submit via the Seguridad Social portal with the three documents above. The approval letter download link arrives by email.

In-person routes:

  • Option 1 — walk-in: any Madrid TGSS Treasury office, no appointment needed. The TA-1 form can be filled in at the office.
  • Option 2 — pre-file online + appointment: file via the portal ("enviar solicitud"), then attend an appointment booked by SMS/email. Critical: on the motivo dropdown, select "Solicitar número de afiliación", NOT "Obtener número de seguridad social", otherwise the request is rejected.

For students in a rush, an IE-partnered law firm can accelerate the process, they get a discount by mentioning IE.

💡 Quick tips

  • Remind students to keep a digital copy of the TA-1 form after filing, it is asked for later in the process.
  • If the student is mid-NIE-renewal, tell them to bring proof of the renewal submission too.
  • Flag the 1-2 week processing time early so students don't assume they can start an internship immediately.
  • For Option 2, double-check the motivo dropdown with the student before they hit submit, the wrong reason auto-rejects the request.

Post-Graduation Options: Masters

What new advisors need to know to guide non-EU Masters graduates through their post-graduation permit options.

What you need to know

Multiple post-graduation permits exist for non-EU IE Masters graduates. Student Services and Talent & Careers give the initial mapping; the student then confirms with a qualified lawyer. Never advise on legal interpretation, your job is to map options and refer.

The seven headline permits and what triggers each:

  • Job Market Immersion (JMI) — for IE-linked internships.
  • Highly Qualified Professionals Permit & EU Blue Card — employer-processed, minimum-salary thresholds apply.
  • Entrepreneur's Residence Permit — requires an approved business plan.
  • Residence Permit (company / self-employment) — open-ended contract or business plan.
  • Residence Permit for Job Seekers — 1 year, non-renewable, mutually exclusive with JMI / internships.
  • Internships not linked to IE — must be applied for within 2 years of graduation.

The official vs. private diploma distinction is the most important filter, it gates almost every recommendation:

  • Official diploma — unlocks the full menu of permits, including Job Seekers.
  • Private diploma — disqualifies students from several options, always check this first.

💡 Quick tips

  • Always verify official vs. private with the academic team before recommending any permit.

Post-Graduation Options: Private Programs

What new advisors need to know to guide graduates of private (non-official) IE programs through a narrower set of post-graduation options.

What you need to know

Private (non-official) diplomas unlock fewer permits than official Masters. The two paths that are not available:

  • No Job Seekers Permit — requires an official Level 6+ EQF degree.
  • No Internships not linked to IE — reserved for official-diploma graduates.

The three viable routes for private graduates:

  • Highly Qualified Professionals Permit — employer-processed contract, minimum salary, proven seniority.
  • Entrepreneur's Residence Permit — online application, solid innovation-focused business plan, 3 years renewable.

What new advisors need to know about permit eligibility for bootcamp graduates.

What you need to know

Bootcamp graduates have similar options to private-program graduates, but eligibility depends on how each bootcamp diploma is legally classified, this is the first thing to check before advising on any permit.

Job Search Residence Permit

What new advisors need to know about the post-graduation job-search permit.

What you need to know

Eligibility hinges on one thing: the degree must be official, Level 6+ EQF. Private diplomas and bootcamps do NOT qualify, students frequently ask, and the answer is no.

The process essentials:

  • Submitted via RED SARA — requires an FNMT digital certificate (no certificate, no submission).
  • ~20 working days processing; administrative silence is treated as approval.
  • Valid up to 12 months from student visa expiration, non-renewable, must be applied for after graduation.

The required documents:

  • EX01 form + fee 790-052.
  • Passport + student visa + student ID.
  • Valid Spanish health insurance covering the full permit period.
  • Official IE diploma, CV and motivation letter.
  • IPREM proof — ≈ 537,84 €/month for the main applicant; +75% for the first dependent; +50% for each additional dependent.

Key nuance students miss: this permit lets them seek work or set up a business, but to actually work they then need a separate work authorisation. Dependents can only accompany if they already did so during the student permit.

💡 Quick tips

  • If a student hasn't started their FNMT digital certificate, flag it immediately, without it they can't submit.
  • Always recommend a specialised immigration lawyer for the submission itself, IE provides guidance, not legal representation.
